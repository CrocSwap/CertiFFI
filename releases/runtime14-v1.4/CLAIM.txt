For the 14 Runtime cases (c00-c13; full, full-PC and constant-time variants), the certified functions in square.elf (sha256 55b0e3bf...), at the reviewed addresses, satisfy their C1 boundary theorems over the canonical GB10 configuration source, on standard GB10 Linux, with no debugger, tracer or hardware profiler attached to the process, for every admissible configuration completion (including MTE2 present or absent), under the premises in the ratified manifest 784d494e. The theorems assume square.elf is started by ordinary execve and that, after its standard startup relocation, the reviewed functions are in memory exactly as reviewed (the loader-image obligations in the manifest, attested only at deployment). Generic-timer state is per-entry data, and the timers are assumed quiet over the execution window (GB10.TimersQuiet). Cases c00-c07 cover each function from entry to its return to the caller. Cases c08-c13 cover a fragment of each function, from entry to a proven stop address; the rest of those functions is not verified. Each result holds only for inputs on the reviewed execution path; executions that take another branch, including the Lean runtime's object-release path, are not covered. The rest of square.elf (the Lean runtime) and the rest of the process are not verified. Every premise set is shown jointly satisfiable at every admissible completion (positive C2). Constant time (CT0, security grades v2.1): for each case, two executions on the same reviewed path whose public inputs (buffer addresses and lengths) agree run to the same final step with equal model event records, and every event of every step of both runs has a record. For c00-c07 the final step is the return to the caller; for c08-c13 it is the proven stop address of the fragment, which is not a return. The event records are the model's explicit accesses by virtual address and size, fetch and table-walk traffic by physical address and size, translation markers and ISB, with no data values; this is a property of the Arm architecture model and says nothing about caches, TLBs or prefetchers. The only proved instance of the two-run premises is a pair of identical runs (identical-run non-vacuity only). Secret residue (CT2.R0, identical-run non-vacuity only): c08 and c11 hold over the fragment to its stop point (every general-purpose register, SP and NZCV returned unchanged per run). The other cases FAIL with these findings: c00-c03, x9 is not proved equal across runs (it last held an address operand); c04-c05, x8 is not proved equal (it depends on a secret input); c06-c07, x8 and x9 are not proved equal (they depend on secret inputs); c09, c10, c12 and c13, x8 is not proved equal at the stop point (a loaded reference count plus or minus one). In c00-c05, x0 is the declared secret output (the boxed byte); c06 and c07 return their input x0 unchanged; c08-c13 have no return value. Also not proved equal across runs: x20 and x29 in c00-c03, x29 in c04-c05, and x20, x21 and x29 in c06-c07 (callee-saved registers whose restore lies outside the proved value plan). Clearing any listed register is the caller's responsibility. Hardware timing independence would additionally require PSTATE.DIT = 1 and Arm's Data Independent Timing guarantee; no current Runtime14 caller sets DIT, so the CT result is a control-flow and access-pattern result only, and the CT1 grade is not claimed. Speculative-execution side channels, including straight-line speculation (SLS), are not addressed: the image is not built with SLS hardening, and no speculation property is claimed. All CT grades are about the architectural (Sail) semantics.
